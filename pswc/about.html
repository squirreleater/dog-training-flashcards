About PSWC

Built on experience.
Driven by development.

The approach

PSWC exists because a lot of dog training is either too vague to test or too heavy to justify. The middle ground — clear mechanics, a plan you can write down, and criteria you can actually fail — is where dogs get built.

Everything runs in the same order. Engagement first, because a dog that will not work with you cannot be trained by you. Then clarity: one behaviour, one marker, one reward event, so the dog knows precisely what paid. Then foundations, built in a room where nothing is happening. Only then does the environment get harder, and it gets harder one variable at a time.

Pressure has a place, but it is applied to a dog that already knows the answer — never as a substitute for teaching it. A dog that is confused and a dog that is refusing look similar from three feet away and require opposite responses. Telling them apart is most of the job.

Why sport and working dogs set the standard

Sport is honest. A routine is judged, a detection dog either finds the odour or it does not, and neither of them cares how good the training looked. Working to that standard means the mechanics have to be real.

That standard does not get lowered for a family dog. A pet owner who wants a reliable recall is asking for the same thing a competition handler is asking for — behaviour that holds when it matters — and deserves the same quality of instruction.

What PSWC will not do

It will not sell a programme to a dog that needs a vet or a behaviourist first. It will not guarantee an outcome on a timeline, because dogs do not run on schedules. And it will not tell an owner their dog is suitable for work it is plainly not built for — that conversation is uncomfortable once and expensive forever if it is avoided.
